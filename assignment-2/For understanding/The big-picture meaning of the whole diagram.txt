Notice the PATTERN: User Service and Catalogue Service are called 
by MULTIPLE other services (User Service gets 2 incoming arrows, 
Catalogue Service gets 2 incoming arrows) — but they never call 
anyone else back.

This makes sense: "who is this user" and "what is this food item" 
are FOUNDATIONAL facts that many other services need to check 
against, but User/Catalogue don't need to know about orders, carts, 
or reviews to do their own job.

This is a normal, healthy pattern in service design — a few 
"foundational" services get called often, while more 
"business-process" services (Order, Cart, Review) call outward 
to gather what they need before doing their own job.

NONE of the arrows go the other direction (e.g. User Service never 
calls Order Service) — this also makes sense: User Service's job 
(managing accounts) doesn't depend on orders existing at all.
